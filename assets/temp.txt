	18 Nov 2024 
	Delhis AQI stood at 494 on November 18 the highest so far this season staying in the ‘Severe category and deteriorating significantly from 441 on November 17. 
Despite steady winds with speeds between 2 and 3 m/s Delhis AQI continues to be severe. There are two reasons for this: 
○ Sentinel-5P satellite data shows plumes of stubble burning from Punjab arriving in Delhi. The stubble-burning season is almost over and farmers are rushing to clear their fields to sow wheat. 
○ Delhi has been under a blanket of fog for more than two days now. Fog reduces the amount of solar radiation that reaches the ground thereby reducing the mixing layer height. Mixing layer height is the height to which pollutants mix vertically in the atmosphere. A low mixing layer height leads to the accumulation of pollutants near the ground. 
The Air Quality Early Warning System (AQEWS) had forecasted a drop in AQI from November 17. However the forecast was incorrect. The system now predicts the citys AQI to fall below 300 from November 19. The wind speed is forecasted to increase beyond 3 m/s and the Ventilation Index (VI) above 6000 m²/s from November 19. Wind speeds above 2.8 m/s and VI above 6000 m²/s are generally favourable for the dispersion of pollutants.

The most probable reason behind the counterintuitive predictions is the underestimation of fire contributions to Delhis pollution in the models. The models use the fire climatology of last year to make predictions on future fire contributions. Last year by November 15 stubble burning was almost over in Punjab. However this year we observed a spike in fire counts as late as November 18.
